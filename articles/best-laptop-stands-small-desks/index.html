Best Laptop Stands for Small Desks

A laptop stand is one of the cheapest ergonomic fixes available, and often the first thing worth buying before a chair or a monitor: it raises your screen toward eye level, which every version of "how to sit at a desk" advice depends on getting right (see our ergonomic setup guide for why eye line matters more than most people assume). The category ranges widely in stability and portability, though, and those two traits usually trade off against each other.

Stability vs. portability is the main trade-off

Fixed-height aluminum stands (a single molded or folded piece) are the most stable and least likely to wobble under typing pressure, but they're bulkier to store or carry. Foldable or adjustable-hinge stands pack flatter and are easier to move between rooms or take on trips, but the hinge points are exactly where wobble shows up over time. If your laptop stays in one spot most of the time, prioritize the stable fixed design; if you move it daily (shared desk, small apartment with no permanent work zone), the portability trade-off is usually worth it.

Angle adjustability matters more than height alone

A stand that only changes height, without letting you adjust the screen's tilt angle, can leave you fighting glare from overhead lighting or a window depending on where your desk sits. Look for a stand with at least a couple of angle settings, not just a fixed riser height.

Ventilation and heat

Laptops vent heat from the bottom or the rear edge, and a solid, non-perforated stand surface can trap that heat rather than help dissipate it. A stand with an open or mesh design under the laptop's vents helps keep fan noise and internal temperatures down during longer work sessions, which matters more for demanding tasks than for basic browsing and documents.

A stand alone doesn't finish the setup

Raising the screen without also raising your typing position tilts your wrists upward, which just moves the strain instead of fixing it. Pair a laptop stand with an external keyboard (and mouse) placed at your normal elbow height — the stand raises the screen, the external keyboard keeps your hands where they belong. If you're running an external monitor instead of or alongside the laptop screen, our monitor arm guide covers the equivalent setup for that case.

A simple decision checklist

  • Fixed stand if the laptop stays put; foldable if it moves often
  • At least basic angle adjustability, not just height
  • Open or ventilated design under the laptop's heat vents
  • Paired with an external keyboard, not raising the screen alone
