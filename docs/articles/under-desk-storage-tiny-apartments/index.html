Under-Desk Storage Ideas for Tiny Apartments

The space under a desk is usually wasted — used for a chair and not much else. In a tiny apartment, that's square footage you can't afford to give up. Here's how to think about using it well.

Map the space before buying anything

Under-desk storage only works if it doesn't block your legs or your chair's rolling path. Measure:

  • The clearance from floor to the underside of the desktop.
  • The distance from the front desk edge to the back wall or baseboard.
  • Whether your chair needs to roll fully under the desk when not in use (if so, leave that path clear and store along the sides instead).

Categories of under-desk storage

Rolling drawer units. Slide in and out from the side, useful for stationery, chargers, and papers. Best when your desk has open legs (not a solid panel) rather than a closed cabinet base, since the drawer unit needs somewhere to sit.

Stackable bins or crates. Cheapest option, flexible sizing, but no protection from dust and no drawers — better for bulk items (cables you're not using, spare hardware) than daily-access items.

Hanging under-desk trays. Mount to the underside of the desktop (usually with clamps, not drilling) to hold a keyboard tray, small tray for a notebook, or a pull-out shelf. Good for reclaiming desktop surface without touching floor space at all.

Cable-specific trays. Not general storage, but worth mentioning here because cable clutter is often what makes under-desk space feel unusable in the first place — see our cable management guide for that specifically.

Matching storage to what you actually keep near your desk

Before buying, sort what currently ends up near your desk into three piles:

  • Daily-use items (headphones, notebook, charger) — these want a drawer or open bin within arm's reach, not sealed storage.
  • Occasional items (extra cables, old hardware, backup drives) — fine in stacked bins further back.
  • Things that don't belong at your desk at all — under-desk storage isn't a substitute for general apartment storage; resist the urge to make it a catch-all.

A note on rented furniture and floor protection

If you're in a rental with hardwood or LVP flooring, check whether rolling storage units have hard casters or soft/rubberized ones — hard plastic casters on hardwood can scratch over time with repeated use.
