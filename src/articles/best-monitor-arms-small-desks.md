A monitor arm's real value on a small desk isn't looks — it's reclaiming the desktop footprint a monitor stand takes up, and letting you push the screen back or up when you need the surface for something else. Here's what actually differs between arms at a given price point.

## Clamp vs. grommet mount

Most monitor arms mount one of two ways: a desk clamp (grips the front and back edge of the desktop, no drilling) or a grommet mount (through a hole in the desktop, permanent). For a rented or [compact desk](/articles/best-compact-desks-small-bedrooms/), clamp mounts are almost always the right call — check the clamp's maximum desktop thickness against your desk before buying, since thin particleboard desks sometimes fall outside the clamp's rated range.

## Weight range matters more than the brand

Every monitor arm lists a supported weight range (e.g., 4.4–19.8 lbs). Check your monitor's actual weight, not its screen size — ultrawide and higher-resolution monitors run heavier than a basic 24" panel. An arm rated below your monitor's weight will sag or fail to hold position; one rated well above it may feel stiff to adjust for a lighter monitor.

## Gas-spring vs. friction arms

Gas-spring arms move with light pressure and hold position at any height — better if you switch between sitting and standing, or share the desk with someone at a different height. Friction arms are cheaper and adequate if you set the height once and rarely change it.

## VESA compatibility

Almost all modern monitors support the VESA 75mm or 100mm mounting pattern on the back, but check yours before buying — some ultra-thin or curved monitors ship without an exposed VESA plate, or need an adapter.

## Single vs. dual arms

A single arm frees the most desktop space for a one-monitor setup. If you're running a monitor plus a laptop, a dual arm (or single arm plus a separate [laptop stand](/articles/best-laptop-stands-small-desks/)) keeps both screens at a consistent eye level, which matters for the same ergonomic reasons covered in our [ergonomic setup guide](/articles/ergonomic-setup-without-full-office-chair/).

## A simple decision checklist

- [ ] Clamp mount confirmed compatible with your desk's thickness
- [ ] Monitor's actual weight checked against the arm's rated range
- [ ] Gas-spring if you adjust height often, friction arm if you don't
- [ ] VESA pattern confirmed on your monitor
- [ ] Single vs. dual decided based on your screen count
